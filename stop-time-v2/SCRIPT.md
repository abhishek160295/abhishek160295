So what if you could stop time?

Weirdly, your brain already fakes a tiny version of this.

Glance at a clock, and the very first second seems to last longer than the rest.

Scientists call it the stopped-clock illusion. Every time your eyes jump, your brain fills the gap and stretches that moment.

And time isn't even the same everywhere.

Clocks on GPS satellites run about thirty-eight microseconds a day faster than clocks on the ground, just as Einstein's relativity predicts.

If engineers didn't correct for it, your phone's map would drift by more than ten kilometers every day.

A fly can see flickering light about four times faster than you can. To a fly, your hand moves in slow motion.

But you don't slow time. You stop it. And in real physics, that's a disaster.

You see because light reaches your eyes. Stop time, and light stops too. Pitch black.

Sound is just air vibrating. Frozen air can't vibrate. Total silence.

And every molecule of air around you would be locked in place, like being sealed inside a wall.

So your power has rules, and you learn them fast.

Rule one. A small bubble moves with you. Inside it, air flows and light reaches you. You can see, and breathe.

Rule two. Anything you touch moves again, but only while you hold it. Let go, and it freezes right where it is.

A basketball hangs over the hoop. Walk home through a frozen rainstorm, and every drop you bump into falls onto you.

Rule three. The world stops. You don't. Every minute in there is a real minute of your life.

You read a whole book. Eat a whole pizza. Nap on the couch. When you let go, the clock hasn't moved. But you're starving.

And there's one thing the rules can't explain.

You show your best friend. One second you're across the room, the next you're beside him.

At first, you use it for the good stuff. Front of the lunch line, every time.

You stop time during a math test and peek at everyone's answers. Perfect score. Again.

At a penalty kick, you nudge the goalie one step to the left.

And when a little kid's ice cream goes flying, you catch it mid-air and put it back in his hand.

Your mom's coffee mug keeps ending up on the ceiling fan.

But the frozen world is strange. No wind. No sound. Birds hang in the sky like they're on strings.

Except, sometimes, they're not where you left them.

It's the quietest place in the universe, and you're the only one in it.

At least, that's what you think.

And rule three adds up. One extra hour a day, for a year, is more than two weeks of life nobody else lived.

You're aging faster than everyone around you, and nobody can see it.

Some nights, one thought keeps you awake. What if you let go, and time never starts again?

Then a kid shoves you into a locker, and everyone laughs.

For the first time, you stop time not for fun, but for revenge.

Nobody can stop you. So it gets easier.
