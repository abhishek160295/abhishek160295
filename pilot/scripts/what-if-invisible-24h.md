# What If You Were Invisible for 24 Hours?

Thumbnail: an empty yellow jacket floating in a dark street, only two eyeballs showing, bus headlights behind. Text: **I'M BLIND.**
Length: ~6:30. Narrator only (Max acts with props, floating clothes and his eyes).
Running gags: (1) **The narrator can hear Max chewing.** (2) **Dave screams at floating objects.** (3) **Sir Biscuit is the only one who always knows where Max is** (he smells him). (4) **The HOUR counter** slams in at every jump.

Key: `VO` = narration · `▶` = visual · `SLO` = slow motion · `FRZ` = freeze frame · `WHIP` = whip-pan cut · `GFX` = graphic

---

## 1. Cold open (0:00–0:25)

VO: [mysterious] This is Max. You can't see him. Nobody can.
▶ An empty yellow jacket and jeans walk down the street. Red shoes, no feet.

VO: [excited] For the next twenty-four hours, Max is invisible.
▶ GFX: a big clock slams in, **24:00:00**, and starts ticking.

VO: Free movies. Free food. Zero consequences.
▶ Fast montage, 0.6 s each: popcorn floating out of a cinema, a donut vanishing bite by bite, a door opening by itself.

VO: [deadpan] Dave has been screaming for six minutes.
▶ Dave backed against a wall, screaming at a floating sandwich.

VO: [pause] There's just one problem.
▶ Hard cut to black. Silence.

VO: Max can't see either.
▶ Two floating eyeballs dart left, right, panicking. A bus horn, far away.

VO: [tense] And by hour nine… it nearly kills him.
▶ Flash, 1 s: headlights filling the frame. SMASH CUT: **HOUR 1 / 24**.

## 2. Hours 1–3: the dream (0:25–1:30)

VO: [excited] Hour one. Max does what everyone says they'd do.
▶ HOUR 1 slams in. Max's clothes swagger into a cinema past the ticket desk.

VO: He watches three films. Back to back. Front row. For free.
▶ GFX: three ticket stubs stamp down. FREE. FREE. FREE.

VO: [sarcastic] Max, I know you're in here. I can hear you chewing.
▶ A dark cinema. A popcorn bucket floats and crunches loudly. Heads turn.

VO: Hour two. Revenge.
▶ HOUR 2. Max sneaks up behind Dave.

VO: He taps Dave on the shoulder. Then the other shoulder. Then both.
▶ Three quick shots: Dave spins left, spins right, spins in a circle.

VO: Dave has now moved house.
▶ A removal van speeds away. Dave's face is pressed against the back window.

VO: Hour three. The ultimate test. Mum.
▶ HOUR 3. Mum in the kitchen. A biscuit lifts off the plate by itself.

VO: [deadpan] Mum does not scream. Mum just says, "Put it back, Max."
▶ FRZ on Mum's flat stare. The biscuit slowly floats back to the plate.

VO: [warm] Some powers are no match for mums.
▶ Max's jacket shrugs.

## 3. The reveal: invisible means blind (1:30–2:30)

VO: [curious] But here's the part every superhero movie gets wrong.
▶ A cinema screen showing a cheesy invisible hero. A big red X stamps over it.

VO: To be invisible, light has to go straight through you. In one side… out the other.
▶ GFX: a beam of light passes through Max's outline, untouched.

VO: But your eyes only work by catching light. The back of your eye has to absorb it to see anything.
▶ GFX: a cartoon eye, light hitting the back, the retina lighting up. Label: CATCH.

VO: Invisible eyes can't catch anything. The light just… keeps going.
▶ The same eye, now invisible. The beam goes straight through and out the back of the head.

VO: [pause] So the second Max turned invisible…
▶ Slow push in on Max's empty jacket.

VO: He went completely blind.
▶ SLO: the world fades to black from the edges in. Silence. A single heartbeat.

VO: [sarcastic] Which explains why he walked into that cinema wall. Twice.
▶ Replay of hour one: the jacket bonks into a wall. Then again.

VO: And it's true in nature too. Every see-through animal on Earth, glass frogs, glass squid, baby eels, still has visible eyes.
▶ GFX: three see-through animals swim and hop in. Their eyes glow bright, circled in yellow.

VO: Because eyes that light passes through… don't work.
▶ The yellow circles pulse. Label: EYES ALWAYS SHOW.

VO: [playful] If you'd still pick invisibility after hearing that, smash like. If not… we're judging you.
▶ Max's floating eyeballs squint at the camera. A like button bounces in and gets pressed by an invisible finger.

## 4. Hours 4–8: blind chaos (2:30–3:50)

VO: Hour four. Max tries to get home.
▶ HOUR 4. The jacket shuffles along with its arms out, feeling the air.

VO: He uses a stick. Then he realises nobody can see the stick's owner, so the stick looks haunted.
▶ A stick taps along the pavement by itself. A crowd screams and scatters.

VO: Hour five. Lunch.
▶ HOUR 5. Max sits at a café table.

VO: [disgusted] Here's something else they don't tell you. Food is only invisible once your body has digested it.
▶ GFX: a sandwich goes in. You can see it sliding down, floating in mid-air, then sitting in his stomach.

VO: So for two hours… Max is a floating, half-chewed sandwich.
▶ A waiter sees a sandwich hovering in a chair. The waiter faints.

VO: Hour six. He's freezing.
▶ HOUR 6. The jacket shivers on a sunny bench.

VO: Sunlight warms you because your skin soaks it up. Max's doesn't. The sunshine goes straight through him.
▶ GFX: sunrays hit a normal person (glows orange, WARM) and pass through Max (stays blue, COLD).

VO: [deadpan] It's thirty degrees. Max is wearing three coats.
▶ The jacket now wears three puffy coats, a scarf and a woolly hat, all floating.

VO: Hour seven. Only one living thing can find him.
▶ HOUR 7. Sir Biscuit sniffs the air.

VO: Sir Biscuit doesn't need to see Max. Sir Biscuit can smell Max. From two streets away.
▶ The dog trots straight to the floating coats, sits, and wags. Max's eyeballs look down, touched.

VO: [warm] Good boy.
▶ An invisible hand pats the dog. The fur flattens and fluffs back up.

VO: [tense] Hour eight. Max is lost, blind, and he has no idea it's getting dark.
▶ HOUR 8. The sky fades orange, then navy. Streetlights flicker on.

## 5. Hour 9: the bus (3:50–4:40)

VO: [tense] Hour nine. Max reaches a road.
▶ HOUR 9 slams in, in red. The coats stand at the edge of a crossing.

VO: He can't see the traffic. And here's the real problem…

VO: The traffic can't see him either.
▶ POV from the bus driver: an empty crossing. The driver hums along to the radio.

VO: [urgent] Max steps out.
▶ The red shoes step off the kerb. SLO.

VO: [shouting] Max!
▶ SLO SET PIECE: the bus fills the frame, the horn stretches into a groan. Orbit around the floating coats. Sir Biscuit barks.

VO: [pause] He's pulled back by the one friend who always knew where he was.
▶ Snap to full speed. Sir Biscuit yanks Max's scarf in his teeth. The coats tumble back onto the pavement. The bus blasts past.

VO: [shaky] Every year, more than a million people die on the world's roads. Being seen is half of staying alive.
▶ GFX: a pedestrian crossing with a big glowing eye above it. Label: SEEN = SAFE.

## 6. Hours 10–24: the loneliest day (4:40–5:40)

VO: [quiet] Hour twelve. Max sits in the park. People walk past. Nobody looks.
▶ HOUR 12. A crowded park, everyone walking past an empty bench.

VO: It turns out being invisible isn't a superpower. It's what lonely already feels like.
▶ GFX: speech bubbles float between people, none of them pointing at Max's bench.

VO: Hour eighteen. Mum leaves a plate out. With a note.
▶ HOUR 18. A plate of biscuits and a sticky note: "FOR MAX. YOU'RE STILL MY SON."

VO: [warm] He eats every single one. You can see all of them.
▶ The biscuits float into his invisible stomach, clearly visible, stacked like a little tower.

VO: Hour twenty-three. Max counts down.
▶ HOUR 23. The clock reads 00:59:59 and ticks.

VO: Three. Two. One…
▶ SLO: Max's outline flickers. Sir Biscuit tilts his head.

## 7. The fix and the twist (5:40–6:15)

VO: [excited] So scientists try to fix him. Keep the invisibility, bring back the eyes. Like a glass frog.

VO: Invisible skin. Invisible muscle. Working eyes.
▶ The dial clicks. A bright flash.

VO: [pause] They forgot about the skeleton.

VO: [deadpan] Mum screamed this time.
▶ Mum screaming, dropping a whole plate of biscuits. Dave faints in the background.

VO: [laughing] Max is now the only person in town who can see perfectly… and still can't go outside.
▶ The skeleton waves sadly from the window.

## Fact check (for the pinned comment)

- Invisibility needs light to pass through you without being absorbed or bent. Seeing needs the retina to absorb light, so a truly invisible eye can't see. (H.G. Wells's *The Invisible Man*, 1897, skips this.)
- See-through animals (glass frogs, glass squid, eel larvae) keep pigmented, visible eyes for this reason.
- Undigested food would be visible inside an invisible body. Wells even used this.
- Skin absorbs sunlight and warms you; if light passed straight through, the sun wouldn't warm you the same way.
- Dogs' sense of smell is tens of thousands of times more sensitive than ours.
- Road deaths: about 1.19 million people a year worldwide (WHO, 2023).
