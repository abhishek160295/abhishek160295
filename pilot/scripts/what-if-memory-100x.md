# What If Your Memory Was 100x Better?

Thumbnail: Max wide-eyed, thousands of memory screens swirling round his head, dark background. Text: **CAN'T FORGET.**
Length: ~8 min (narration ~6.3 min plus slow-mo and beats). Narrator only (Max acts with his face, signs and props, so there are no second-voice credits).
Running gags: (1) **The Wave**: in 2014 Max waved back at someone who was waving at the person behind him. (2) **Keys**. (3) **Dave's dog, Sir Biscuit**. (4) The narrator forgets things and Max can't.

Key: `VO` = narration · `▶` = visual · `SLO` = slow motion · `FRZ` = freeze frame · `WHIP` = whip-pan cut · `GFX` = graphic

---

## 1. Cold open (0:00–0:35)

VO: [excited] This is Max. Max just read his entire chemistry textbook.

VO: Once. In the bathroom.
▶ FRZ on his face. Toilet roll spins.

VO: And now he knows every word. Every diagram. The coffee stain on page two hundred and twelve.
▶ SLO: pages fly out of the book and into his eyes, one by one. The coffee stain lands last.

VO: Exam? Four minutes. One hundred percent.
▶ WHIP to the exam hall. Max slams down his pen. A score counter rolls up to 100, sparks fly.

VO: [deadpan] The teacher had to sit down.

VO: Because this morning, Max's memory got one hundred times better.
▶ GFX: a brain icon with a dial cranking from 1x to 100x. CLANG.

VO: Which sounds amazing. And for about a week… it is.
▶ Montage, 0.5 s shots: Max high-fiving, Max on a podium, Max winking.

VO: [beat] Then it ruins his life. Slowly. In alphabetical order.
▶ Hard cut to black.

VO: [tense] By day thirty, Max will beg to forget. And one memory… nearly gets him killed.
▶ Flash-forward, 1.5 s: a bus horn, headlights filling the frame, Max frozen mid-road. SMASH CUT to TITLE: **CAN'T FORGET.**

## 2. How good is 100x? (0:35–1:20)

VO: [curious] So how good is "a hundred times better"?
▶ Max holding a tape measure up to his own head.

VO: Here's you. You learn something today… and by tomorrow, about two thirds of it is gone.
▶ GFX: a full water glass labelled TODAY. Overnight it drains to a third. Label: TOMORROW.

VO: That's not you being lazy. That's normal. It's called the forgetting curve, and everyone's on it.
▶ GFX: a curve sliding downhill. Little stick people ski down it, screaming.

VO: Max? Max isn't on the curve. Max is a straight line. Forever.
▶ Max calmly walks along a flat line while the stick people crash past below.

VO: The world's top memory athletes can learn a shuffled deck of cards in about fifteen seconds.
▶ SLO: 52 cards fan through the air around a memory athlete in a headband.

VO: Max does it before you've finished shuffling.
▶ Max yawns, recites them. The athlete drops his headband.

VO: And your brain can hold a lot. By one estimate, around two and a half petabytes.
▶ GFX: a brain shaped like a hard drive. A loading bar fills.

VO: That's roughly three million hours of TV. Max intends to use all of it.
▶ GFX: a stack of TVs shooting up past the clouds. Max on top, eating popcorn.

## 4. The first crack (2:30–3:30)

VO: [quiet] Here's something nobody tells you. At night, your brain doesn't just rest. It cleans up.
▶ GFX: inside a cartoon brain, tiny janitors with brooms sweep away glowing memory cards.

VO: It sorts the day, keeps what matters… and quietly bins the rest.
▶ Janitors toss cards into a bin labelled "NAH".

VO: What you had for lunch three Tuesdays ago? Binned. What that stranger was wearing on the bus? Binned.
▶ Two quick cards fly into the bin: a sandwich, a hat.

VO: Max's brain doesn't have a bin.
▶ The janitors look around. No bin. One shrugs.

VO: So everything stays. Every face, every sound, every second.
▶ Dutch tilt. Cards pile up to the ceiling of the brain. The janitors are buried.

VO: Including… the Wave.
▶ FRZ. Ominous music sting.

VO: [slow] March 2014. A woman waved. Max waved back.
▶ Flashback, washed-out colour: young Max waving happily.

VO: She was waving at the guy behind him.
▶ SLO: the camera orbits round the frozen moment. The guy behind waves back. Max's hand is still up.

VO: You've forgotten your version of this. Max can't. He relives it in full HD. Surround sound. Every night.
▶ Max in bed, eyes open, the Wave projected on his ceiling like a cinema. A "NOW SHOWING" sign.

VO: [deadpan] It now has a director's cut.
▶ The ceiling projection adds dramatic lighting and a lens flare.

## 5. The science twist (3:30–4:45)

VO: [curious] And this isn't made up. There was a real man like Max.
▶ A sepia photo frame slides in: a 1920s silhouette with a question mark.

VO: Russia, the 1920s. A journalist named Solomon Shereshevsky. His boss noticed he never took notes.
▶ An old newspaper office. Everyone scribbling except one man with folded arms.

VO: So scientists tested him. Long lists of numbers, words, nonsense. He remembered them all. Then he remembered them again… fifteen years later.
▶ GFX: a list of numbers. A calendar flips 15 years in 2 seconds. The same list, perfect. A green tick STAMP.

VO: Sounds like a superpower. But here's what the scientist studying him noticed.
▶ Zoom punch on the scientist's glasses.

VO: Solomon struggled to recognise faces.
▶ Max looking at his mum. Her face flickers through smiling, frowning, laughing.

VO: Because a face is never the same twice. Smiling, frowning, different light. He remembered every version… so none of them matched.
▶ GFX: six slightly different versions of Mum's face in a grid. Max's eyes dart between them. A big red "NO MATCH".

VO: [beat] Max's mum is not thrilled.
▶ Mum with arms crossed, tapping her foot.

VO: And that's the big secret. Forgetting isn't your brain failing. Forgetting is your brain working.
▶ SLO: a giant FORGETTING stamp hits a document. Dust explodes.

VO: When you forget the details, you keep the pattern. You don't remember every dog you've ever met. You just know… "dog".
▶ GFX: a hundred dogs blur and merge into one simple dog icon.

VO: Max remembers every dog. Individually.
▶ Max surrounded by a hundred dogs, sweating.

VO: So when he sees a new one… his brain just goes "error".
▶ A new dog walks in. Max's eyes go to a spinning loading wheel.

VO: [beat] Except Sir Biscuit. He'd know Sir Biscuit anywhere.
▶ Sir Biscuit walks past in sunglasses. Max nods.

VO: [tense] Funny, right? It stops being funny on a Tuesday. At a crossing.
▶ FRZ on Max at a red pedestrian light. A clock ticks. Cut.

## 6. The meltdown and the near miss (4:45–6:40)

VO: [tense] Week three. Max's head is full of everything, all the time.
▶ Dutch tilt. Max at his desk. Tiny memory screens orbit his head like a swarm.

VO: Someone says "sandwich", and his brain plays every sandwich he's ever eaten. In order. With the receipts.
▶ Fast montage, 0.3 s each: sandwiches slam in like stamps, faster and faster.

VO: He can't read a joke, because a joke needs you to forget the obvious meaning first.
▶ Max holding a joke book. The punchline floats above, and he stares at it blankly.

VO: [sarcastic] Comedy is dead to him. Which is a shame, because this video is hilarious.
▶ Cut to the empty mic stand. A single sad trombone.

VO: He can't make simple choices. Pick a restaurant? He remembers every meal he's had at every one of them.
▶ GFX: a split screen of 40 restaurant menus. A decision bar fills to 1%, then crashes.

VO: [tense] Then comes that Tuesday. Max steps off the kerb, and the green man reminds him of a green jumper.
▶ Max mid-step at the crossing. The green man icon glows.

VO: Which reminds him of a birthday. Which reminds him of a cake. Which reminds him of every cake. Ever.
▶ Speed ramp: memory screens slam over his eyes one after another, faster and faster, until the street disappears.

VO: [urgent] He's not looking at the road any more. He's looking at two thousand and nine.
▶ POV: Max sees only a sunny 2009 party. Faint and distorted underneath: a horn.

VO: [shouting] Max!
▶ SLO SET PIECE: the bus fills the frame, the horn stretches into a low groan. Camera orbits Max frozen mid-road, memories still swirling, the bus one metre away.

VO: [beat] Real life comes back at the very last second.
▶ Snap to full speed. Max stumbles back onto the kerb. The bus blasts past and his hair whips sideways.

VO: [shaky] Your brain filters out the past so you can see the present. Max's can't.
▶ GFX: two lenses. YOU: a clear view of the road. MAX: the road buried under a hundred floating memories.

VO: [quiet] And the worst part: bad memories don't fade.
▶ The lighting goes cold. Max alone on a bench.

VO: For you, an embarrassing day turns into a funny story. Time sands the edges off.
▶ GFX: a jagged rock tumbling in a river, coming out smooth.

VO: For Max, the edges never come off. Every argument, every goodbye, every awkward silence stays sharp.
▶ The same rock comes out still jagged and slices the river in half.

VO: [heavy] One woman who lives with a real version of this, called hyperthymesia, described it as "nonstop, uncontrollable, and totally exhausting".
▶ GFX: an infinity sign made of tiny memory screens, looping. No face shown.

VO: Scientists have only confirmed a few dozen people like her. Most of us will never meet one.
▶ GFX: a world map. A few dozen tiny lights blink across the globe.

VO: [building] And then comes the night it all happens at once.
▶ Max in bed. The ceiling projection flickers on by itself.

VO: Every memory he's ever had. All playing. At the same time.
▶ SLO SET PIECE: the camera pulls back as thousands of screens burst out of Max's head and fill the room, then the house, then the street. Freeze-frame orbit. Silence. Then everything plays at once at full volume.

VO: [whisper] Including the Wave.
▶ In the middle of the chaos, one screen in perfect focus: the Wave.

## 7. The fix (6:40–7:40)

VO: [gentle] So what does Max actually want?
▶ Max at dawn on the rooftop, hollow-eyed, holding a mug.

VO: Not a better memory. A worse one. Just a little.
▶ GFX: the 100x dial from the start. Max slowly turns it back down. Click… click… click.

VO: Because here's what you've got, right now, for free: a brain that keeps what matters…
▶ GFX: a sieve. Big glowing gems (MUM, BEST DAY, FIRST BIKE) stay in.

VO: …and lets the rest slip away.
▶ Sand pours through: TRAFFIC, ADVERTS, a tiny 2014 Wave falling through the holes.

VO: That's how you get over things. That's how you learn rules instead of lists. That's how a bad day becomes a funny story.
▶ Three quick shots: a heart stitching itself up, a rulebook replacing a giant list, a frown flipping into a laugh.

VO: Forgetting isn't a bug. [beat] It's the best feature you've got.
▶ SLO: the sieve shines like a trophy. Max holds it up, low hero angle, sunrise behind.

## Fact check (for the pinned comment)

- Forgetting curve: Hermann Ebbinghaus, 1885. Without review, roughly two thirds of new material is gone within a day.
- Card memorisation: top memory athletes learn a shuffled deck in roughly 15 seconds or less.
- Brain capacity ~2.5 petabytes: an estimate by Paul Reber (Northwestern), widely quoted, not a measurement.
- Solomon Shereshevsky: studied for about 30 years by neuropsychologist Alexander Luria (*The Mind of a Mnemonist*, 1968). He recalled lists after 15+ years and struggled with faces and abstract or figurative language.
- Hyperthymesia (HSAM): first described in 2006 (Parker, Cahill, McGaugh) in the case of Jill Price, who called her memory "nonstop, uncontrollable, and totally exhausting". A few dozen confirmed cases.
- Forgetting helps thinking: Richards & Frankland, *Neuron* (2017), "The Persistence and Transience of Memory".
- Sleep and memory sorting: during sleep the brain consolidates important memories and weakens unimportant connections.
