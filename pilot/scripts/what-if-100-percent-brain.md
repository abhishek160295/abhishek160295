# What If You Used 100% of Your Brain?

Thumbnail: a glowing brain-city at night, every window lit, sparks flying off it, a meter at 100% in red. Text: **100%** (white), **BRAIN** (yellow).
Length: ~6:30 (narration ~5:50 plus holds and set pieces). Narrator speaks to the viewer ("you").

**New format**
- **Outside = you.** First-person point of view: your hands, your phone, your room, your street. The viewer is the hero. No Max.
- **Inside = the brain city.** A huge city at night inside your skull. Districts are real brain areas: the **Eye Tower** (vision), the **Memory Library** (hippocampus), the **Alarm Station** (amygdala), **Word Street** (language) and the **Control Room** (frontal lobe). Tiny glowing neuron workers pass sparks along wires.
- **The meter.** A brain meter on screen climbs through five levels: 25% → 50% → 75% → 90% → 100%. Each level gives a power and a cost. More of the city lights up each time.
- **The truth twist:** the "10% of your brain" idea is a myth. You use all of your brain, just never all at once. At 100%, every light comes on together and the city blacks out.

**Characters (new)**
- **Neil**, the foreman neuron. Hard hat, tiny clipboard, permanently tired. He complains about overtime at every level.
- **Biscuit the cat.** Your cat. Sleeps through everything. Completely unimpressed by your genius.

**Retention engine**
- **Open loop, 0:15 → 5:00:** "At 100%, something happens that no movie ever shows you." It's paid off at the blackout.
- **The meter is a visible progress bar.** Viewers stay to see 100%.
- **Every level is a new set piece.** The stakes grow from bedroom to café to park to live TV.
- **One "wait, really?" fact per level**, all real.

Running gags: (1) **Neil and his overtime.** (2) **The cat does not care.** (3) **The internet guy who says "Actually, it's a myth"** (and is right, which annoys you).

Key: `VO` = narration · `▶` = visual · `SLO` = slow motion · `FRZ` = freeze frame · `GFX` = graphic

## 1. Cold open (0:00–0:35)

VO: [excited] You wake up. And something is very, very different.
▶ First-person view: ceiling, blink, alarm clock. Everything looks sharper, brighter.

VO: [curious] You can hear the fridge downstairs. You can hear your own heartbeat. You can count the threads in your blanket.
▶ Fast cuts: a fridge, a heartbeat line, a blanket in extreme close-up with numbers popping over every thread.

VO: [whisper] Last night, you took a tiny pill from a very suspicious website. And now… your brain is switching on. All of it.
▶ A tiny pill on the bedside table. A brain meter slides onto the screen and starts climbing.

VO: [tense] Movies love this idea. Use a hundred percent of your brain, become a genius. But at a hundred percent, something happens that no movie ever shows you.
▶ Flash-forward: the brain city, every window lit… then every light goes out. Black.

VO: [sarcastic] Don't worry. We'll get there. Probably.
▶ SMASH CUT to TITLE: **100% BRAIN**

## 2. Inside the city (0:35–1:20)

VO: [curious] First, let's look inside. This is your brain. A city of eighty-six billion neurons.
▶ The camera flies through your ear into the brain city at night. Towers, streets, wires glowing.

VO: Every neuron is a tiny worker passing sparks to its neighbours. That's a thought. Sparks, passed along. Billions of times a second.
▶ Close on neuron workers passing glowing sparks hand to hand along a wire.

VO: [playful] This is Neil. Neil is a neuron. Neil has worked here since before you were born. Neil is tired.
▶ Neil: hard hat, clipboard, enormous yawn. A tiny coffee mug.

VO: [curious] And here's the weird part. On a normal day, most of the city is dark. Only small groups of lights switch on at any moment. A district for seeing. A district for remembering. A district for being scared of spiders.
▶ GFX: the city at night with only a few districts lit, each with a label: SEE, REMEMBER, SPIDERS.

VO: [tense] But not today. Today, the whole city is waking up.
▶ Lights begin flicking on across the skyline. Neil drops his coffee. The meter: 25%.

## 3. Level 1, 25%: Senses wide open (1:20–2:05)

VO: [excited] Twenty-five percent. You walk downstairs, and you notice… everything.
▶ POV: the kitchen. Labels pop over everything: the number of cereal flakes, the temperature of the toast, a spider in the corner of the ceiling.

VO: Every word on the cereal box. Every crack in the ceiling. The exact moment the toast is about to pop.
▶ Three quick cuts, the last one: the toaster pops and you catch the toast mid-air without looking.

VO: [curious] And this part is real. Your eyes send your brain millions of bits of information every second. But scientists estimate you only consciously notice a tiny trickle of it. Your brain throws the rest away so you don't go mad.
▶ GFX: a fire hose of dots pours into the Eye Tower. A tiny cup at the bottom catches a few drops, labelled YOU.

VO: [deadpan] Today, your brain stopped throwing it away.
▶ The cup overflows. Dots flood the street.

VO: [curious] And here's something you've never noticed. There's a hole in your vision right now. Every eye has a blind spot, where the nerve leaves the eye. Your brain simply paints over it. Today, you can see the hole.
▶ GFX: an eye with the nerve leaving the back, a small black gap in the view. A tiny neuron painter brushes over it, then the paint peels off.

VO: [playful] You also remember where you left everything. The TV remote. Your first tooth. Every password you've ever forgotten.
▶ POV: you reach under the sofa without looking and pull out a remote, then a tiny box with a tooth, then a sticky note covered in passwords.

VO: [sarcastic] Your cat is staring at you. Your cat is not impressed.
▶ Biscuit the cat, half asleep on the counter, one eye open. Closes it.

## 4. Level 2, 50%: Speed learning (2:05–2:55)

VO: [building] Fifty percent. You open a book. You finish the book. It took forty seconds.
▶ POV: pages flicking so fast they blur. A stopwatch: 0:40. Inside: the Memory Library's shelves slamming full.

VO: There was a real man named Kim Peek who could read two pages at once. One with each eye. He remembered almost everything he read.
▶ GFX: an open book, two eyes, two arrows, one to each page. Label: 1 PAGE PER EYE.

VO: [excited] By lunch, you've learned to juggle, speak Italian, and play the piano. Badly, then brilliantly.
▶ Fast cuts: juggling oranges, a phrasebook, piano keys, the last shot perfect and dramatic.

VO: [deadpan] There's just one problem. You are starving.
▶ POV: your stomach growls loud enough to rattle a glass.

VO: [curious] Because your brain is greedy. It's only about two percent of your body weight, but it burns about twenty percent of your energy. Running on roughly twenty watts. About the same as a dim light bulb.
▶ GFX: a body silhouette with the brain glowing. A bar: 2% WEIGHT next to a bar: 20% ENERGY. A dim light bulb labelled 20 W.

VO: [sarcastic] At fifty percent, you've eaten nine sandwiches. Neil has started a union.
▶ A tower of sandwich crusts. Inside the city: Neil holding a tiny protest sign that says OVERTIME.

## 5. Level 3, 75%: Pattern genius (2:55–3:45)

VO: [excited] Seventy-five percent. Now you start seeing patterns everywhere.
▶ The meter clicks to 75%. Half the city now blazing.

VO: You know what people will say before they say it. You finish the waiter's sentence. You finish his next sentence too. He leaves.
▶ A café. A waiter opens his mouth; your speech bubble appears first. He slowly backs away.

VO: [building] In the park, a chess champion is beating everyone. You beat him in four moves. Without looking.
▶ SLO: you move a piece with your eyes closed. The champion's face. A crowd gasps.

VO: [deadpan] A pigeon lands next to you. You calculate its flight path, its mood, and what it had for lunch.
▶ A pigeon with dotted trajectory lines, a mood meter, and a tiny bread icon.

VO: [tense] But the patterns don't switch off. You see faces in the clouds. Faces in the toast. Your socks are judging you.
▶ Fast cuts: a cloud with a face, toast with a face, two socks with angry eyebrows.

VO: [curious] That's real too. It's called pareidolia. Your brain is so hungry for patterns, especially faces, that it finds them where there aren't any. Everyone does it. Today, you can't stop.
▶ GFX: a power socket that looks like a shocked face. Label: PAREIDOLIA.

VO: [playful] Actually, quick question. How many faces did you just see in this room? Tell us in the comments.
▶ POV pans across a room full of hidden faces: the clock, the lamp, the window.

## 6. The myth (3:45–4:25)

VO: [sarcastic] And now, at this point, someone in the comments is typing: "Actually, we use a hundred percent of our brain already."
▶ A phone screen. A comment box. A guy with glasses types very fast.

VO: [deadpan] And annoyingly… they're right.
▶ FRZ on the comment. A big green tick.

VO: [curious] The "ten percent" idea is a myth. Nobody knows exactly where it started, but brain scans show that over a normal day, you use pretty much all of your brain. Every district does a job.
▶ GFX: a brain scan lighting up region by region across a clock face, until the whole brain has been lit at some point.

VO: The difference is, you never use it all at the same time. At any moment, only a small slice of your neurons are firing. That's not laziness. That's the design.
▶ Back in the city: districts switching on and off in turns, like a perfectly timed light show.

VO: [tense] But today, the districts aren't taking turns. They're all staying on.
▶ The meter jumps to 90%. The light show stops. Every district stays lit. Wires start to glow red.

## 7. Level 4, 90%: Too much (4:25–5:05)

VO: [building] Ninety percent. Someone books you on live TV. The biggest quiz show in the country.
▶ A TV studio. Spotlights. A glittering set. A host with huge teeth. Your POV from the contestant's podium.

VO: [excited] You answer every question before the host finishes reading it. Capital of Mongolia. Ulaanbaatar. The atomic number of gold. Seventy-nine. What the host had for breakfast. Eggs. He's furious.
▶ Fast cuts, a buzzer on every cut, the score climbing. The host's eye twitches.

VO: [building] The audience is chanting your name. Your mum is texting you in all capitals.
▶ The crowd on its feet. A phone buzzing: a text in capitals with seven exclamation marks.

VO: [tense] But inside your head, the city is on fire. Every memory is playing at once. Every song you've ever heard is stuck in your head at the same time.
▶ Inside: the Memory Library's books flying off shelves, Word Street jammed with traffic, music notes swarming every street.

VO: [urgent] Neil is running. Neil has never run.
▶ Neil sprinting down a red-lit street, hard hat flying off, clipboard flapping.

VO: [tense] Final question. For one million.
▶ The host holds up a golden card. The meter creeps: 95… 98… 99…

## 8. Level 5, 100% (5:05–5:40)

VO: [whisper] One hundred percent.
▶ SLO: the meter hits 100%. Inside the city, every single light comes on at once. The whole skyline blazes white.

VO: [quiet] And this is the part the movies never show you.
▶ Hold on the blazing city. Silence.

VO: [tense] When every neuron fires at once, it isn't genius. It's a power cut.
▶ Every light in the city goes out. Total black. Hold for a beat.

VO: [curious] In real life, when huge groups of neurons fire together all at once, that's what a seizure is. The brain's signals stop making sense, because nothing is taking turns anymore.
▶ GFX, gentle and clear: two rows of lights. Top row: lights blinking in turns, a smooth wave, labelled NORMAL. Bottom row: all lights flashing together, labelled ALL AT ONCE.

VO: [warm] Seizures are a real medical condition that lots of people live with, and doctors can really help. But they show the big secret of your brain. It works because it doesn't switch everything on.
▶ The city's lights come back on, slowly, a few at a time, in their normal rhythm.

## 9. Waking up (5:40–6:10)

VO: [quiet] You wake up on the studio floor. The whole country just watched.
▶ POV: looking up at studio lights. The host leaning over you. A worried crowd.

VO: [warm] The pill has worn off. You're back to normal. Which, it turns out, was a hundred percent all along.
▶ The meter fades out. Inside: the city in its normal rhythm, districts lighting and resting in turns.

VO: [deadpan] You did not win the million. But you can still speak Italian. Mostly.
▶ You on the bus home, reading an Italian phrasebook, looking confused.

VO: [playful] At home, the cat is asleep. The cat has been using its brain perfectly all day.
▶ Biscuit the cat asleep in a sunbeam. One eye opens. Closes.

VO: [warm] So here's the truth. Your brain isn't lazy. It isn't ten percent. It's an eighty-six-billion-worker city that knows exactly when to rest.
▶ The brain city at dawn, calm, perfectly lit in patches. Neil asleep at his desk, a blanket over him.

VO: [playful] If you could switch on one district to a hundred percent, just for a day, which one would you pick? Comments.

## Fact check (for the pinned comment)

- The 10% myth: brain imaging shows that over the course of a day, virtually every part of the brain is active. There is no silent 90%. The myth's origin is unclear; it's often wrongly linked to William James or Einstein.
- About 86 billion neurons in the human brain (Azevedo et al., 2009).
- Only a small fraction of neurons fire at any one moment. Energy limits alone mean the brain can't run everything at once (Lennie, Current Biology, 2003).
- The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy, roughly 20 watts.
- The eyes send the brain millions of bits per second; estimates for what we consciously process are tiny by comparison.
- Kim Peek, the real-life inspiration for the film Rain Man, could read two pages at once, one with each eye, and remembered most of what he read.
- Pareidolia: seeing faces and patterns in random things like clouds, toast and sockets. It's normal and universal.
- A seizure happens when large groups of neurons fire together in an abnormal, synchronised burst. Epilepsy is common and treatable; anyone worried should talk to a doctor.
