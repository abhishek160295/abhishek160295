# What If You Could Read Minds?

Thumbnail: Max in a crowd, glowing eyes, thought bubbles everywhere, staring horrified at one bubble. Text: **THEY HATE ME?!**
Length: ~6:30. Narrator only. Thought bubbles do the talking (text and icons).
**Spine:** Max wakes up able to read minds and finds a note: "I can read minds too. Midnight." Every scene is Max reading a suspect to find out who wrote it. The answer arrives in the last minute.
Running gags: (1) **The midnight clock** jumps in between acts. (2) **Dave is terrified of ducks.** (3) **The narrator begs Max to stay out of HIS head.** (4) **Sir Biscuit only ever thinks FOOD** (it's a clue).

Key: `VO` = narration · `▶` = visual · `SLO` = slow motion · `FRZ` = freeze frame · `GFX` = graphic

---

## 1. Cold open: the note (0:00–0:35)

VO: [excited] This is Max. This morning, Max woke up able to read minds.
▶ Max in bed, eyes snapping open. His eyes glow. A thought bubble pops above Sir Biscuit at the foot of the bed: "FOOD."

VO: [deadpan] His first ever mind-read was the dog. It said "food." Great start.
▶ FRZ on Max's unimpressed face.

VO: [tense] Then he found this under his door.
▶ SLO: a folded note slides under the bedroom door. Max unfolds it.

VO: [whisper] "I can read minds too. I know what you're thinking. Midnight."
▶ Close-up of the note: wobbly handwriting, a mystery smudge in the corner. A clock face appears: 08:00, ticking toward 12.

VO: [tense] Someone else has the power. Someone who knows him. And Max has until midnight to find out who.
▶ Fast cuts, 0.5 s each: Mum, Dave, the teacher, a girl on a bus. Each face gets a red question mark.

VO: [pause] Luckily, he can read minds. Unluckily… so can they.
▶ SMASH CUT to TITLE: **THEY HATE ME?!**

## 2. Suspect one: the girl on the bus (0:35–1:20)

VO: [excited] Suspect one. Zoe. She sits across from Max on the bus every single morning.
▶ The bus. Zoe (WOMAN outfit) reading. A SUSPECT 1 card slams in.

VO: Max focuses. And for the first time in his life, he hears what a girl actually thinks about him.
▶ Max's eyes glow. A bubble slowly inflates above Zoe's head.

VO: [whisper] "…he's cute."
▶ FRZ. Hearts burst around Max. Low trumpet fanfare.

VO: [deadpan] Not exactly a criminal mastermind. Suspect one, cleared.
▶ A red pen crosses Zoe off a suspect board. Max is still floating, grinning.

VO: [excited] Nine a.m. The maths exam. Max doesn't need to study. The teacher is literally thinking the answers.
▶ Exam hall. The teacher's bubble: "Q4 = 17." Max writes 17. "Q5 = B." Max writes B.

VO: [sarcastic] One hundred percent. Again. The teacher is now thinking a word we can't put on YouTube.
▶ The teacher's bubble fills with black scribble and a bleep.

VO: But it can't be him. His brain is too busy wondering where he left his car.
▶ The teacher's bubble: a car park, a question mark. Crossed off the board.

## 3. The first rule: every brain speaks its own language (1:20–2:10)

VO: [curious] Lunch. Max tries to read a stranger. And gets… nothing.
▶ A café. A stranger eating soup. Max squints. The bubble above them is pure TV static.

VO: [curious] Here's why, and it's real. In 2023, scientists in Texas built an AI that turns brain scans into sentences.
▶ A lab. A big brain scanner hums. A volunteer lies inside, listening to headphones.

VO: But it only worked after learning each person's brain for about sixteen hours. And a decoder trained on one brain… was useless on anyone else.
▶ GFX: two brains glowing in completely different patterns. A key fits one and bounces off the other. Label: 16 HOURS EACH.

VO: Every brain speaks its own language. So the people Max knows best are the only ones he can really read.
▶ GFX: a circle of faces. The stranger is static. Mum, Dave and Zoe light up clearly.

VO: [tense] Which means whoever wrote that note… knows him very, very well.
▶ The note, close-up. Slow push in. The clock: 13:00.

VO: And even then, people don't think in neat sentences. Scientists who sampled random moments of people's thoughts found actual words only about a quarter of the time.
▶ GFX: a pie chart. One quarter labelled WORDS. The rest: a picture frame, a heart, a blank square labelled "…".

VO: [deadpan] The rest is pictures, feelings, and a lot of people thinking about absolutely nothing.
▶ A supermarket aisle. Forty bubbles, all showing the same thing: a big question mark.

## 4. Suspect two: Dave (2:10–3:20)

VO: [tense] Suspect two. Dave. Best friend. Knows everything about Max.
▶ Dave's front door. A SUSPECT 2 card slams in. Dave opens the door a crack.

VO: Max reads him. And hears…
▶ Max's eyes glow. A bubble above Dave fills with music notes.

VO: [deadpan] "Baa baa black sheep, have you any wool…"
▶ Dave staring straight at Max, smiling, the nursery rhyme looping in his bubble.

VO: Max reads harder. "Yes sir, yes sir, three bags full."
▶ The bubble's music notes speed up. Max sweats.

VO: [tense] Dave is blocking him. On purpose. Which is exactly what a mind reader would do.
▶ FRZ on Dave's innocent smile. A red circle appears around his face on the suspect board.

VO: [curious] And here's the thing, this is real too. In that same Texas study, people could beat the decoder just by thinking about something else on purpose. Counting. Naming animals.
▶ GFX: a brain scan screen spitting out garbled words while a volunteer silently counts 7, 14, 21…

VO: [playful] So if a mind reader ever comes for you, start counting by sevens. You're welcome. And if that just saved your secrets, smash like.
▶ Max glares at the camera. A like button bounces in and gets pressed.

VO: [sarcastic] And Max, if you're listening, please stay out of MY head. It's private in there.
▶ Max puts his ear to the narrator's microphone stand. The narrator's bubble: a rubber duck.

VO: [pause] …It's not about Dave.
▶ FRZ. Max slowly raises an eyebrow.

VO: Max spends nine hours outside Dave's house. Dave sings "Baa Baa Black Sheep" for all nine.
▶ A time-lapse: the sun crosses the sky. Dave at the window, still mouthing the song. Max's eye twitches.

VO: [tense] Then Dave cracks. One tiny thought slips through.
▶ The bubble glitches. Music notes fall away.

VO: [whisper] "…please don't let him find out about the ducks."
▶ The bubble: a duck. Dave screams and slams the door.

VO: [deadpan] Dave is not a mind reader. Dave is just terrified of ducks. Suspect two, cleared.
▶ Dave crossed off. The clock: 21:00.

VO: [tense] Nine p.m. Max goes to the big match to clear his head. Bad idea. Sixty thousand people.
▶ A giant stadium. A counter rolls to 60,000.

VO: Your brain is brilliant at picking out one voice in a noisy room and ignoring the rest. It's called the cocktail party effect.
▶ GFX: a party of sound waves. One glows yellow; the others fade grey.

VO: [tense] But thoughts don't come through your ears. There's nothing to filter.
▶ The same GFX. Every wave turns red at once.

VO: [building] Kick-off. Sixty thousand minds switch on.
▶ The whistle. SLO SET PIECE: thousands of thought bubbles burst from every seat and swirl around Max like a tornado.

VO: "Goal!" "Hungry." "Did I leave the oven on?" "Why is that man screaming?"
▶ Fast cuts, 0.4 s each: bubbles slamming in front of Max. The last one shows Max himself, screaming.

VO: [sarcastic] Even the team mascot is thinking about quitting.
▶ A giant duck mascot. Its bubble: "I hate this job." Dave, in the stands, faints.

VO: [urgent] Eleven fifty-eight. Two minutes to midnight. One suspect left.
▶ The clock: 23:58, red, ticking loudly. Max runs out of the stadium.

## 6. Suspect three: Mum (4:10–5:15)

VO: [quiet] Max gets home. The kitchen light is on. Mum is waiting.
▶ The kitchen at night. Mum sitting at the table with two cups of tea. A SUSPECT 3 card slides in, slowly.

VO: She knows when he's lying. She knows when he's sad. She always has.
▶ Quick memory flashes: Mum catching young Max with a biscuit, Mum hugging him after a bad day.

VO: [tense] Max takes a breath. And reads his mum's mind properly, for the first time in his life.
▶ SLO: Max's eyes glow. The camera pushes slowly into Mum's bubble.

VO: [warm] And he doesn't find a mind reader. He finds… himself.
▶ Inside the bubble: a reel of memories. Baby Max. Max's first steps. Max's first terrible haircut.

VO: Every birthday. Every scraped knee. The first time he said "Mum."
▶ Memories flutter past like pages. Each one glows warm gold.

VO: [quiet] And here's something real about memory. Every time you remember something, your brain rebuilds it. A little different each time.
▶ GFX: a memory card being taken apart and rebuilt, the colours shifting slightly.

VO: [warm] So Mum doesn't remember Max exactly as he was. She remembers him a little better. Every single time.
▶ The same memory of young Max, now glowing brighter, the haircut looking slightly less terrible.

VO: [pause] Mum didn't write the note. Mum doesn't need superpowers. She's been reading him his whole life.
▶ Mum slides a cup of tea across the table and smiles. Max's eyes water.

VO: [tense] Then… the clock strikes midnight.
▶ The clock: 00:00. A single bell chime. Every light in the house flickers.

## 7. The reveal (5:15–6:00)

VO: [whisper] Something scratches at the back door.
▶ SLO: the back door creaks open. A shadow in the doorway. Low and small.

VO: [tense] It walks in. Sits down. And stares straight into Max's soul.
▶ Sir Biscuit, in silhouette, then lit up. He sits. He tilts his head.

VO: [pause] It was the dog.
▶ FRZ. The note lies next to Sir Biscuit. The mystery smudge on it is a paw print.

VO: [sarcastic] How did a dog write a note? We're not answering that.
▶ Sir Biscuit with a crayon in his mouth. He drops it, very casually.

VO: [curious] But a dog reading minds? That part's real. Dogs can tell a happy human face from an angry one. They follow where we look and where we point. Their brains even react to the emotion in our voices.
▶ GFX: a dog looking at two faces, a smile and a frown, and picking the smile. Then a dog following a pointing finger to a ball.

VO: Sir Biscuit has been reading Max for years. Every mood. Every lie. Every time he was sad.
▶ Quick callbacks: Sir Biscuit staring at Max in bed, at the bus stop, at Mum's kitchen table.

VO: [laughing] And what was he thinking the whole time? The answer was the very first thought Max ever read.
▶ Sir Biscuit's bubble slowly inflates.

VO: [deadpan] "FOOD."
▶ FRZ on the bubble. Mum laughs. Max laughs. Sir Biscuit wags.

## Fact check (for the pinned comment)

- Brain-to-text decoding: Tang et al., Nature Neuroscience, 2023 (University of Texas at Austin). An fMRI decoder recovered the gist of stories after about 16 hours of training scans per person; a decoder trained on one person failed on others; and people could resist it by deliberately thinking of other things (counting, naming animals).
- Inner speech frequency: Descriptive Experience Sampling studies (Russell Hurlburt and colleagues) found inner speech in roughly a quarter of random moments.
- The cocktail party effect: Colin Cherry, 1953.
- Memory reconsolidation: recalled memories become changeable and are re-stored (Nader et al., 2000, and later work).
- Dogs and human faces: dogs can discriminate happy from angry human faces (Müller et al., Current Biology, 2015), follow human pointing and gaze, and their brains respond to emotional tone in human voices (Andics et al., 2014).
