# What If You Could Hear Everyone's Thoughts?

Thumbnail: Max in the middle of a crowd, dozens of thought bubbles around him, staring horrified at one. Text: **THEY HATE ME?!**
Length: ~6:00 (narration ~5:05 plus holds). Narrator only (Max acts with his face, thought bubbles do the talking as text and icons).
Running gags: (1) **"…he's cute."** The crush's thought from the opening, paid off at the end. (2) **Dave's secret:** he's terrified of ducks. (3) **The narrator begs Max not to read HIS mind.** (4) **Sir Biscuit only thinks FOOD.**

Key: `VO` = narration · `▶` = visual · `SLO` = slow motion · `FRZ` = freeze frame · `GFX` = graphic

---

## 1. Cold open (0:00–0:30)

VO: [excited] This is Max. And this morning, Max heard something nobody was supposed to hear.
▶ Max on a bus. A girl across the aisle glances at him. A thought bubble pops above her head.

VO: [whisper] "…he's cute."
▶ FRZ on Max's face. Hearts burst. Low trumpet fanfare.

VO: [excited] Because today, Max can hear what everyone is thinking. Every person. Every thought. All the time.
▶ Fast montage, 0.5 s each: thought bubbles popping above a bus driver, a dog, a baby, a pigeon.

VO: [sarcastic] Exams? Solved. Poker? Solved. What his friends really think of his haircut?
▶ Dave smiling politely. His bubble: a scissors emoji and a skull.

VO: [pause] Solved. Unfortunately.
▶ Max's smile slowly drops.

VO: [tense] By the end of today, Max will hear sixty thousand people at once. And one thought will change everything.
▶ Flash, 1 s: a stadium, thousands of bubbles exploding outward. SMASH CUT to TITLE: **THEY HATE ME?!**

## 2. The dream (0:30–1:30)

VO: [excited] Nine a.m. The maths exam.
▶ Exam hall. Max sits back, arms crossed, totally calm.

VO: Max doesn't need to study. The teacher is literally thinking the answers.
▶ The teacher's bubble: "Q4 = 17." Max writes 17. Bubble: "Q5 = B." Max writes B.

VO: [sarcastic] One hundred percent. Again. The teacher is now thinking a word we can't put on YouTube.
▶ The teacher's bubble fills with black scribble and a bleep.

VO: Lunchtime. Poker with Dave.
▶ A cardboard-box table, chips, Dave in sunglasses.

VO: Dave has a perfect poker face. And a thought bubble that says "two of clubs, two of clubs, please don't notice."
▶ Dave's bubble shows a sad little two of clubs. Max pushes all his chips in.

VO: And then Max finds out Dave's deepest, darkest secret.
▶ Slow push in on Dave's bubble.

VO: [deadpan] Dave is terrified of ducks.
▶ The bubble: a duck. Dave's real face: sweating. A duck waddles past. Dave jumps onto the table.

VO: [warm] For one morning, Max knows everything. He's never felt more powerful.
▶ SLO: Max walking down the street, low angle, bubbles floating around him like a crown.

VO: [excited] Two p.m. A job interview. For a job he is not qualified for.
▶ An office. A stern interviewer (TEACHER outfit) flips through a very short CV.

VO: The interviewer asks, "What's your biggest weakness?" And thinks, "please don't say perfectionism."
▶ The interviewer's bubble: the word PERFECTIONISM with a big red X.

VO: [deadpan] Max says "honesty." He gets the job. He starts Monday. He doesn't know what the company does.
▶ A handshake. FRZ on Max's confused smile. A sign behind him reads "SALES?"

VO: [curious] Three p.m. Max discovers something about people. They lie. A lot.
▶ A busy street. Every second bubble has a little red "LIE" tag on it.

VO: In one well-known study, people kept a diary of every lie they told. On average? One or two a day. Mostly small ones.
▶ GFX: a calendar where every day gets one or two small red stickers. Labels: "I'm fine." "Love the haircut." "On my way!"

VO: [sarcastic] "On my way!" Max can hear that they are, in fact, still in the shower.
▶ A man texting "On my way!". His bubble shows him in the shower with a rubber duck.

## 3. The first crack (1:30–2:20)

VO: [quiet] Then he goes home for dinner.
▶ Mum's kitchen. Mum proudly puts down a plate of something grey.

VO: Mum says, "Do you like it?" Max says, "Mmm, delicious." Max thinks, "this is a crime." And Mum thinks…
▶ Split screen: Max's bubble (a crime scene) and Mum's bubble, still loading…

VO: [pause] "…he hates it. He's always hated it. Since he was four."
▶ Mum's bubble: a little memory of four-year-old Max spitting peas. Mum's smile stays perfectly still.

VO: [deadpan] Max eats the whole plate. Twice.
▶ Max shovelling grey food, eyes watering.

VO: And here's what nobody warns you about. People don't think in nice, clean sentences.
▶ GFX: a perfect speech bubble with neat words, crossed out.

VO: Scientists who studied this asked people to write down what was in their heads at random moments.
▶ GFX: a beeper goes off. A tiny person freezes and scribbles on a notepad.

VO: Actual words, an inner voice, showed up only about a quarter of the time.
▶ GFX: a pie chart. One quarter labelled WORDS lights up.

VO: The rest? Pictures. Feelings. And a surprising amount of… nothing.
▶ The other slices fill with icons: a picture frame, a heart, and a blank square labelled "…"

VO: [sarcastic] So mostly, Max isn't reading minds. He's reading static.
▶ Max's view of a crowded street: every bubble is TV static with the occasional word: "lunch", "ugh", "where are my keys".

VO: [deadpan] Ten minutes in a supermarket, and Max now knows forty people forgot why they walked in.
▶ A supermarket aisle. Forty bubbles, all showing the same thing: a big question mark.

## 4. The people with no voice (2:20–3:10)

VO: [curious] Then Max meets someone strange.
▶ An office. A woman (WOMAN outfit) typing calmly. No bubble above her head.

VO: He listens. And listens. And hears… absolutely nothing.
▶ Max leaning in, ear almost touching her head. Silence. A cricket chirps.

VO: [tense] Is she plotting something? Is she a robot? Is she… immune?
▶ Fast cuts: Max's imagination bubbles: a spy, a robot, a superhero with a shield.

VO: [pause] No. She's just one of the people who don't have an inner voice at all.
▶ The woman turns, smiles and waves. Normal.

VO: This is real. Researchers gave it a name only recently: anendophasia. Some people live their whole lives without that little voice in their head. And many of them didn't know everyone else had one.
▶ GFX: two heads side by side. One with a little speech bubble inside, one with a calm empty space. Labels: VOICE / NO VOICE.

VO: [playful] So quick question. Do YOU hear a voice in your head right now? Reading this to you?
▶ Max turns to the camera and squints at the viewer. A bubble pops above the camera: "?".

VO: Tell us in the comments. You'll be amazed how many people don't.
▶ A comment box slides in and fills up fast with YES, NO, WAIT WHAT.

VO: And if this just blew your mind a little, smash like. We'll pretend we didn't hear what you were thinking.
▶ A like button bounces in and gets pressed. Max smirks.

## 5. Real mind reading (3:10–3:50)

VO: [curious] Here's the scary part. Scientists are already getting close to this.
▶ A lab. A big brain scanner hums. A volunteer lies inside.

VO: Your brain has around eighty-six billion neurons, all firing in patterns. A thought is one of those patterns.
▶ GFX: a brain made of tiny glowing dots. Patterns ripple across it like city lights at night.

VO: In 2023, a team in Texas trained an AI on brain scans while people listened to stories for hours.
▶ GFX: a brain lighting up in patches while a podcast plays. Waves of colour.

VO: Afterwards, it could turn new brain activity into rough sentences. Not word for word. But the gist.
▶ GFX: a screen types out a fuzzy sentence. Some words wobble and swap.

VO: [deadpan] It only works if you lie still in a giant magnet for sixteen hours. So your thoughts are safe. For now.
▶ The volunteer, very bored, inside the scanner. A clock spins.

VO: [sarcastic] And Max, if you're listening, please stay out of MY head. It's private in there.
▶ Max puts his ear to the microphone stand. The narrator's bubble: a rubber duck.

VO: [pause] …It's not about Dave.
▶ FRZ. Max slowly raises an eyebrow.

## 6. The stadium (3:50–5:00)

VO: [tense] Evening. Dave gets two tickets to the big match.
▶ Outside a giant stadium. Dave waving tickets. Max nervous.

VO: Sixty thousand people.
▶ GFX: a counter rolling up to 60,000. A crowd icon multiplies.

VO: Your brain is amazing at one thing here. It can pick out one voice in a noisy room and ignore the rest. It's called the cocktail party effect.
▶ GFX: a party of people with sound waves. One wave glows yellow; all the others fade grey.

VO: [tense] But thoughts don't come through your ears. There's nothing to filter. Max gets all of them.
▶ The same party GFX. Every wave turns red at once.

VO: [building] Kick-off. Sixty thousand minds switch on.
▶ The whistle blows. SLO SET PIECE: thousands of thought bubbles burst from every seat at once, swirling up into a tornado around Max.

VO: [sarcastic] Even the team mascot is thinking about quitting.
▶ A giant duck mascot on the pitch. Its bubble: "I hate this job." Dave, in the stands, faints.

VO: "Goal!" "Hungry." "Did I leave the oven on?" "I hate this team." "I love this team." "Why is that man screaming?"
▶ Fast cuts, 0.4 s each: bubbles slamming in front of Max's face, one per line. The last one shows Max himself, screaming.

VO: [urgent] And then, in the middle of all of it, one thought. Clear as a bell.
▶ Everything freezes mid-air. Silence. One bubble glows, drifting toward Max.

VO: [whisper] "…he's cute."
▶ SLO: Max turns. Three rows back, the girl from the bus.

VO: [pause] She's looking straight past him.
▶ The camera follows her eyes. She's looking at Sir Biscuit, sitting in Dave's lap, in a tiny team scarf.

VO: [deadpan] She was always talking about the dog.
▶ FRZ on Max's face. Sad trombone. Sir Biscuit's bubble: "FOOD."

## 7. The meltdown (5:00–5:40)

VO: [quiet] Max leaves the stadium. But the noise follows him home.
▶ Max walking alone at night. Bubbles drift from every window he passes.

VO: Neighbours. Babies. Dogs. Dave, dreaming about ducks.
▶ Quick shots: a baby's bubble (milk), a dog's bubble (FOOD), Dave asleep with a duck bubble, sweating.

VO: [heavy] He can't sleep. He can't think. He can't even hear his own thoughts any more.
▶ Max in bed, pillow over his head, hundreds of bubbles packed around the bed like balloons.

VO: And that's the twist. When you hear everyone… you lose the only voice that was actually yours.
▶ GFX: Max's own little bubble getting smaller and smaller, buried under the others.

VO: [quiet] He tries a library. Silent, right? Wrong. Three hundred people quietly thinking very loud thoughts.
▶ A library. Everyone reading silently. Above them, a ceiling packed with bubbles: "is this boring", "I'm hungry", "who smells of onions".

VO: He tries the woods. Birds, squirrels, a very anxious deer.
▶ A forest. A deer's bubble: a car's headlights. A squirrel's bubble: "NUT NUT NUT."

VO: [heavy] The only quiet place left is next to the woman from the office. The one with no inner voice.
▶ Max sitting on a bench beside her in total silence. For the first time all day, his shoulders drop.

## 8. The fix (5:40–6:15)

VO: [warm] So here's what Max learns. Not knowing what people think isn't a bug. It's what makes people kind.
▶ Morning. Mum's kitchen. Mum puts down the grey dinner again.

VO: It's why Mum still cooks for him. It's why Dave still plays poker with him.
▶ Max eats it. Mum smiles. Dave waves through the window holding two of clubs.

VO: Everybody thinks something weird sometimes. You just don't have to hear it.
▶ GFX: a crowd. Each bubble closes softly like a door.

VO: [laughing] Oh, and Max finally works up the courage to talk to the girl from the bus.
▶ Max walks up to her in the park, nervous.

VO: She thinks he's cute too. He'll never know for sure. And honestly? That's the best part.
▶ She smiles at him. No bubble. Max smiles back. Sir Biscuit's bubble pops up between them: "FOOD."

## Fact check (for the pinned comment)

- Inner speech frequency: Descriptive Experience Sampling studies (Russell Hurlburt and colleagues) found inner speech in roughly a quarter of random moments; inner seeing, feelings and "unsymbolized thinking" were about as common.
- Anendophasia (little or no inner speech): named in a 2024 study by Nedergaard & Lupyan (Psychological Science).
- Brain-to-text decoding: Tang et al., Nature Neuroscience, 2023 (University of Texas at Austin). An fMRI-based decoder recovered the gist of stories, not exact words, after about 16 hours of training scans per person.
- The cocktail party effect: Colin Cherry, 1953. Your brain can focus on one voice in a noisy room.
- Lying diaries: DePaulo et al., 1996. Participants told about one to two lies a day, mostly small social ones.
- About 86 billion neurons in the human brain (Azevedo et al., 2009).
