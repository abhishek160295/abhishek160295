## Pinned comment

The real science in this one:

- Your brain takes roughly a tenth of a second to process what your eyes see, so it predicts where moving things will be to make up for the delay. It's one explanation for the flash-lag illusion.
- A fast tennis serve reaches the other player in about 0.4 to 0.5 seconds. Returning it is prediction, not reaction.
- One hour is 3,600 seconds, so Max's power is about 36,000 times your brain's.
- In 1961, Edward Lorenz re-ran a weather simulation with 0.506 instead of 0.506127 and got a completely different forecast. He later called it the butterfly effect.
- A 5-day forecast is right about 90% of the time. A 10-day forecast is right only about half the time (NOAA).
- Déjà vu isn't seeing the future. One leading idea is that it's your brain's memory checker noticing a small conflict. It's common in healthy people.
- In a 2016 study (de Berker et al., Nature Communications), people were more stressed by a 50/50 chance of a small shock than by knowing for sure it was coming.
- Hot-air balloons can't steer. Pilots change height to catch winds blowing in different directions.
