# What If You Were 50x Faster? (v2)

Runtime target: ~9 min (≈1,600 words of VO)
Narrator: Asher or Arthur (pick one, then it's the channel voice)
Character: Max (same model as the demo)

`[V]` = visual, `[SFX]` = sound, `CARD` = on-screen number card. Lines in `{braces}` are delivery tags for the narrator (eleven_v4) and are never shown on screen. Everything else is voiceover.

---

## Narration plan

- **One narrator, one personality.** A dry, amused friend explaining something ridiculous, not a documentary voice. He likes Max, and he's slightly worried about him.
- **Record in sections, not one long take.** Each `##` block below is one generation. A weak line then costs one section to redo, not the whole episode. Pick the best take per section.
- **Rhythm rules** (already written into the lines):
  - A big number is always followed by a beat, then a comparison you can picture (a pickup truck, a Big Mac, an egg white).
  - Punchlines get their own short line after a pause. Never bury a joke mid-sentence.
  - The narrator mostly asks the viewer's question out loud ("So why...?") before answering.
- **Tags we use:** `{amused}`, `{dry}`, `{deadpan}`, `{conspiratorial}`, `{short pause}`, `{beat}`, `{quieter}`, `{low, ominous}`. Max is never voiced. He reacts with eyes and body only, so there's no lip-sync.
- **Pronunciation:** "km/h" is said as "kilometres an hour". Numbers are written as words in the final TTS text.
- **Mix:** VO always on top; music ducks −8 dB under speech; master to −14 LUFS.

## 0:00 Cold open

[V] Max at a bus stop. Bus doors close in his face. Bus pulls away.

Max missed the bus. Again.

{amused} Not a problem. As of this morning, Max is fifty times faster than you.

[V] Max crouches. Legs become a cartoon blur, feet spinning on the pavement, smoke, and he goes... nowhere. Then slowly, slowly, he starts moving.

{beat} Which raises a question.

[V] Bus overtakes the bus stop sign. Max overtakes the bus. Two and a half hours later, Max arrives at work, drenched, collapsing at his desk.

If Max is fifty times faster than you, why does his commute now take longer than yours?

{conspiratorial} We did the maths. It's worse than you think.

[V] Title card: WHAT IF YOU WERE 50x FASTER?

---

## 0:35 The rules

[V] White void. Rule cards pop in next to Max.

Quick ground rules.

Max's body gets the upgrade. Muscles, bones, tendons, reflexes. Everything moves fifty times faster, and it's tough enough not to rip itself apart doing it.

Everything else stays normal. His brain. His stomach. His skin. His weight.

And the air around him.

{dry} Keep an eye on that list. Every single thing on it is coming back for him.

---

## 1:00 On paper

[V] Max walking next to a speedometer that spins up.

So how fast is fifty times?

A normal walk is about five kilometres an hour. Max's walk would be two hundred and fifty.

CARD: Walk 5 → 250 km/h

A light jog, five hundred. Faster than any passenger train running today.

A proper sprint: about twelve hundred and fifty kilometres an hour.

{beat} The speed of sound is twelve hundred and thirty-five.

CARD: Sprint 1,250 km/h vs sound 1,235 km/h

On paper, Max can sprint through the sound barrier.

{dry} Hold on to that phrase. "On paper."

---

## 1:30 The fun part

[V] Fast montage. Each beat gets a stopwatch in the corner.

Problems later. First, the good stuff.

Washing up, normally fifteen minutes. Max does it in eighteen seconds.

The whole flat hoovered in under a minute.

A ten-minute shower takes him twelve seconds. Well, the scrubbing does. The water still falls at normal speed, so mostly Max just stands there, waiting for it to land on him.

[V] Max at a keyboard, hands a blur.

He can type two thousand words a minute. {beat} Which sounds great, until you remember his brain didn't get the upgrade. He types way faster than he can think, so every email is just whatever was in his head at the time.

[V] Inbox: "RE: RE: why did you send me a picture of a goose"

{deadpan} Max has had a strange week at work.

---

## 2:25 The high-five

[V] Coworker Dave raises a hand. Slow motion.

Then there are other people.

Dave goes in for a high-five. A normal hand moves at maybe five metres a second. Max's moves at two hundred and fifty.

At that speed, a high-five lands with about the energy of a heavy sniper round.

[V] Freeze frame on contact. Cut away. [SFX] distant car alarm.

That's why the upgrade matters. A normal hand would shatter. Max's hand is fine.

{deadpan} Dave's hand has been better.

So Max learns rule number one. Around people, move like you're carrying a full cup of tea. Always.

---

## 3:00 What really sets your top speed

[V] Navy line-art card: sprinter on a treadmill, force arrows under the foot.

Here's something real. In 2010, a team at Southern Methodist University put sprinters on a treadmill that measures every push their feet make. They wanted to know what actually sets the human speed limit.

It isn't how hard your muscles push. It's how fast. A sprinter's foot is only on the ground for about a tenth of a second, and that's all the time you get to shove the planet backwards.

Max's foot touches the ground for two thousandths of a second. No human muscle fires that fast. That's exactly the part of Max we upgraded.

So, problem solved. Max can sprint at twelve hundred and fifty...

{beat} {dry} ...no he can't.

---

## 3:30 Problem one: grip

[V] Close-up: Max's shoe on the road. Force arrows. The shoe slips.

When you run, you push the ground backwards, and the ground pushes you forwards. But it can only push as hard as your shoes can grip. And grip depends on one thing: how heavy you are.

Max didn't get heavier.

So no matter how fast his legs move, his shoes can only shove him forward with about the force of a fifty-kilo bag of sand. Push any harder, and his feet just spin.

[V] Road Runner-style spinning feet, smoke. This time with a little physics label: "friction limit".

Meanwhile, the faster he goes, the harder the air pushes back. And at about one hundred and seventy kilometres an hour, the air pushes back exactly as hard as his shoes can push forward.

CARD: Real top speed ≈ 170 km/h

That's Max's real top speed. Fifty times faster legs, and he runs about four times faster than Usain Bolt.

And his acceleration? Grip again. Nought to a hundred takes him about three and a half seconds. {beat} The same as a decent sports car. Not bad. Not superhero.

[V] Max with a huge backpack full of tins. Speedometer climbs past 240.

There is a cheat. More weight means more grip, and the air doesn't care what's in your bag. Strap on your own body weight in tinned beans, and Max's top speed jumps to about two hundred and forty.

{amused} The fastest Max will ever run, he's carrying seventy kilos of beans.

---

## 4:45 Problem two: the air

[V] Max mid-run, cheeks flapping, a hurricane symbol on screen.

Even at one-seventy, the air is brutal. That's a Category 2 hurricane blowing straight into his face, for as long as he's running.

And this is the bit almost everyone gets wrong. Going twice as fast doesn't take twice the power to fight the air. It takes eight times. Power goes up with the cube of your speed.

CARD: 2x faster = 8x power. 50x faster = 125,000x power.

If Max somehow did hit twelve hundred and fifty, he'd need about ten megawatts just to shove the air aside. That's about what a high-speed train's motors put out. From one guy. In trainers.

Even at his real top speed, he's putting out more power than a small car engine. And that has to come from somewhere.

---

## 5:25 Problem three: lunch

[V] Max opens the fridge. It's empty. He looks at the camera.

For Max, that somewhere is food.

Muscles are only about a quarter efficient, so every bit of push costs him four times as much in fuel. Running flat out, Max burns about six hundred calories for every kilometre.

CARD: ~600 kcal per km

His commute is five kilometres. That's three thousand calories. About five Big Macs, before he's sat down.

And remember, his stomach didn't get the upgrade. It digests at normal speed. So even if he eats the Big Macs, they're not arriving in his muscles any time soon.

His muscles carry roughly two thousand calories of quick fuel on board. At full speed, that's gone in about a minute.

{beat} Luckily for Max, something else gets him long before that.

---

## 6:10 Problem four: the heat

[V] Thermometer on screen. Max sweating. Then steaming.

Remember the quarter-efficient muscles? The other three quarters of that energy doesn't vanish. It turns into heat. Inside Max.

Running flat out, his body temperature climbs by about a third of a degree every second.

CARD: +0.36 °C every second

Ten seconds of running, and he's at forty-one degrees. That's a dangerous fever. Twenty seconds, and he's at the point where people die of heatstroke.

And the human body can only get rid of about a kilowatt of heat, by sweating. Nobody's body does better.

So for every ten seconds Max runs, he has to lie down in the shade for about fifteen minutes to cool off.

[V] Map of Max's commute: 10 short dashes, 10 long naps. A timer reads 2h 30m. A normal pedestrian strolls past and arrives first.

Which means his five-kilometre commute is ten little sprints, and about two and a half hours of lying on pavements.

{deadpan} Walking would take an hour.

{beat} So Max learns the most important rule of being fifty times faster: mostly, don't be.

---

## 7:05 Problem five: brakes, corners and his brain

[V] Max hurtling down a street. A corner coming.

Even ten seconds is risky.

Stopping comes down to grip again. Air resistance helps a bit, but from top speed Max still needs about a hundred metres to stop.

Corners are worse. To turn at full speed without sliding, he needs a turning circle more than half a kilometre wide. He can't turn onto his own street. He overshoots the whole block.

[V] Max's head with slow, normal-speed gears inside.

And then there's the brain. Normal speed. It takes a person about a quarter of a second to react to something they see. At top speed, Max covers twelve metres in that time, completely blind.

Add that to his hundred metres of braking, and he needs to spot trouble more than a football pitch away.

{dry} Max isn't a superhero. He's a missile with anxiety.

---

## 7:45 Where the speed does show

[V] Park. Max holds a tennis ball. A dog waits, tail wagging.

But here's the thing. Grip only limits how hard Max can push sideways along the ground.

His arms don't need grip.

He throws the ball for the dog. A decent throw is about a hundred kilometres an hour. Max's is roughly four times the speed of sound.

[SFX] sharp crack. [V] The ball streaks off, glowing at the front.

At that speed, the air hitting the front of the ball heats up to nearly a thousand degrees. The felt starts to scorch before it's even left the park.

{deadpan} The dog has not got it back yet.

[V] Max crouching, looking straight up.

And nothing limits how hard he can push down. So Max can jump. Straight up, about two hundred and ninety metres, which is higher than the top deck of the Eiffel Tower.

He did it once. That one jump burned six hundred calories and put his temperature up by eight degrees.

{deadpan} He did it once.

---

## 8:30 The rescue

[V] A quiet street. A kid next to Max chases a ball into the road. A car comes round the corner at 50 km/h. The driver is looking at their phone.

Which is why, when it finally matters, Max doesn't run.

He couldn't anyway. His grip gives him the acceleration of a sports car, and the car is already too close.

So he spends a quarter of a second being useless while his brain catches up. Then he scoops the kid up, gently, like she's made of glass.

And he goes up.

[V] Max and the kid rise two metres. The car passes underneath. The ball bounces off its bumper. They land softly on the pavement.

Not fast. Not far. Just a careful two-metre hop that she'll remember as a fun bounce.

{quieter} The driver never even saw them.

---

## 9:00 The verdict

[V] Max on a park bench, eating a family-size sandwich, dog asleep next to him.

So, is being fifty times faster worth it?

For chores, emails, badly, and catching your phone before it hits the floor, absolutely.

But for actually getting anywhere, the grip, the air, the food and the heat all point to the same answer. The fastest Max can keep going without cooking himself is about eighteen kilometres an hour.

{beat} The speed of a decent jogger.

Fifty times faster, and he's stuck at your pace. Because speed was never the hard part. Getting rid of the heat is.

## 9:30 Cliffhanger + outro

[V] Night. Max's doormat. An official-looking letter slides through the door. Generic seal, not a real agency.

Max has one more problem.

Twenty-three people reported sonic booms coming from the park this week.

And somebody would like a word.

[V] Dark room. A figure watching CCTV of a glowing tennis ball crossing the sky. Pauses the frame.

{normal} So, what would you do with fifty times speed? Best answer in the comments goes into a future episode.

And if you want to see what happens when somebody goes faster than Max... a lot faster... subscribe. We're working up to it.
