# Inspired Daily: audience profile

This is inferred, not measured. It is based on the genre, the reference creator's audience pattern, our posting markets and the language of the comments these reels attract. Check it against YouTube Studio (Audience → Age, Gender, Geography) and update this file.

## Who watches
- **Age 16–28, peaking 18–24.** Students and early-career people.
- **Male-leaning, about 65/35.** Discipline, gym and "grind" content skews male; the emotional and family reels pull more women.
- **Geography:** India first (the largest Shorts market and the creator's own market), then the US, Philippines, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria and the UK. English-first, globally readable.
- **Where they are in life:**
  - School and college students in exam culture: boards, entrance exams, semesters, drop years.
  - New graduates job hunting.
  - People in their first job who moved to a new city.
  - Gym beginners.
  - People trying a side hustle.

## What they struggle with daily (most relatable first)
1. **Phone:** "just 5 minutes" turns into 3 hours. Scrolling instead of studying or working.
2. **Exams:** cramming the night before, the "group study" that becomes a group chat, fear of results, failing and the drop year.
3. **Comparison:** cousins and friends getting jobs, cars and weddings first. Relatives asking "what are you doing now?"
4. **Family:** parents' sacrifice (Dad working double shifts, worn-out shoes), wanting to make them proud, the guilt of letting them down, the first salary going to parents.
5. **Job hunt:** dozens of applications, rejection emails, the first offer.
6. **Gym consistency:** packed in January, empty by March.
7. **Grind after work:** the 9-to-5 vs the "5-to-9" side project.
8. **Loneliness:** alone in a new city or hostel, calling Mom, missing home.

## What they share
- "That's literally me" moments: they tag friends and comment "1" or "2".
- Parent and sacrifice stories, which they send to family and save.
- Comebacks after failure (results, rejections).

## Rules for ideas
- Every scene must be a moment this audience has lived **this week**, then the reference metaphor on top.
- Use universal props: phone, books, exam hall, result screen, gym, office, laptop, small rented room, parents. No country-specific names; "your cousin", "relatives" and "Dad" travel everywhere.
- Everything else follows the reference bible:
  - Monochrome, with gold for the win and red for the loss.
  - A principle line and A vs B panels.
  - Counters, crowds and one strong final line.
