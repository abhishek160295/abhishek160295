01 · The DNA

Why these reels go viral

Every one of the 7 reels is the same machine: two characters receive the same conditions and make different choices, and the ending shows the result of those choices without a single word of explanation. The viewer works out the lesson themselves, which is why they rewatch, save and share.

R1

Two characters, same conditions.Same bricks, same 5 minutes, same rain, same diving board. Only the choice changes. If the conditions differ, the lesson collapses.

R2

The lesson is shown, never explained.The final frame proves the point: the wall is built, the man is on top, the water is stored. Text only names it.

R3

Under 16 seconds.The references run 3 to 16 s. Aim for 10 to 14 s. Short means the completion rate is above 100% because people loop it.

R4

Labels, not sentences.OVERTHINKER / DOER. PERFECTIONIST / ACTION TAKER. Two words tell the viewer who to watch.

R5

Universal pain point.Snoozing, overthinking, following the crowd, being mocked. Each viewer should see themselves in the losing character and want to be the winning one.

R6

Language free.No voiceover, simple English labels. That makes the reel work in every country, which multiplies reach.

R7

One visual system.Black stage, white hero, grey crowd, one red object. Recognisable in the feed before anyone reads a word.

02 · Script

The 5-beat structure

Write every reel on this grid before you draw anything. Times are for a 12-second reel at 30 fps.

0.0–1.0sHook frameBoth characters already on screen, labels visible, action already started. Never start on empty black.
1–3sSame startShow the shared condition: the bricks, the alarm, the rain.
3–8sSplitChoices diverge. The winner repeats a simple action; the loser stalls, loops or mocks.
8–11sPayoffThe result lands visibly: wall complete, top of the tower, drought. Hardest SFX hit here.
11–13sName itOne line of text on a hold frame that loops cleanly back to the start.

Script template

FieldFill inExample (Overthinker vs Doer)
PairLoser label / winner labelOVERTHINKER / DOER
Shared conditionSame object, same momentTwo identical diving boards over water
Winner actionOne repeatable actionJumps, swims, climbs back, jumps again
Loser actionOne stalling loopSteps up, crouches, steps back
Red objectThe symbol of the problemRed tip of the board (the edge he won't cross)
Payoff frameThe still that proves itDoer swimming freely, overthinker still dry
Closing lineMax 8 words, optionalNone needed. The labels do the work.

Test before you animate: describe the reel in one sentence with no labels. If a stranger can't guess the lesson from that sentence, the visual metaphor is too weak. Change the metaphor, not the text.

03 · Visual

The visual rule book

Canvas

  • 1080 × 1920, 9:16, 30 fps. Render H.264, 10–15 Mbps.
  • Keep all action in the middle 1080 × 1100 (y 380 to 1480). Instagram covers the top 250 px and bottom 400 px with UI.
  • Labels sit at y 420 to 520, above the characters, never at the very top.

Palette

#000000 stage
#F2F2F2 hero
#8A8A8A loser / crowd
#3A3A3A props, floor
#E5322D one red object
V1

Red appears on one object only.Spirit level, blindfolds, flag, sun, alarm light. Red marks the thing the lesson is about.

V2

Hero is brighter than everyone else.White, with a soft glow (outer glow, 6–10 px, 30% opacity). The loser or crowd sits at 55–65% brightness.

V3

Glossy floor.Mirror the scene under the ground line at 15–20% opacity with a fade. That single trick makes flat stickmen look premium.

V4

Vignette and grain.Radial vignette at 40%, film grain at 3–5%. Keeps pure black from looking like a dead screen.

V5

Losing ending = fade to dark.The losing side desaturates and dims in the last second (crowd fading in the climb reel, red sun dimming in the umbrella reel).

V6

Small watermark.Your handle in the label font, 18 px, 12% opacity, centred under the floor line. Visible but never distracting.

Stickman spec

Solid circle head, one stroke weight for every limb, round caps, no hands or feet. Head diameter = 1 unit; torso and legs about 1.6 units each. Stroke = 22% of head diameter. Characters are always drawn side-on so the action reads at phone size.

Typography

  • Font: a squared pixel or mono caps face such as Silkscreen, Space Mono Bold or VT323.
  • Labels 30–34 px, letter-spacing +12%, all caps, white at 90%.
  • Closing line 32–38 px, centred, max 2 lines, max 8 words per line.
04 · Motion

Animation rules

M1

Speed is the story.The winner moves at a steady, quick rhythm. The loser moves slower, stops and repeats. Viewers read the lesson from tempo before they read the labels.

M2

Loops for the loser.Animate the stalling action once (2 to 3 s) and cycle it. The repetition is the joke and the lesson.

M3

Progress for the winner.Something must accumulate: wall height, distance climbed, water level, a timer. Visible accumulation is what makes the payoff satisfying.

M4

Ease in and out on everything, except impacts.Use ease in/out curves on movement, and hard stops on contact (brick lands, feet hit the water), with 2 frames of squash.

M5

Anticipation before every big move.Crouch 6 frames before a jump, lean back 4 frames before a throw.

M6

Hold the payoff 1.5 s.Let the result frame breathe before text arrives. This is where people screenshot.

M7

Seamless loop.The last frame should cut back to the first without a jump. The easiest method is to end on a pose and layout that match the opening frame.

M8

Camera stays still.A locked camera, with at most one slow push-in (100% to 108%) during the payoff.

05 · Sound

Sound design rule book

Music comes from Instagram, so the sound you bake into the video is effects only. Done well, it makes cheap animation feel expensive, and it means the reel still works when someone watches without the music.

The four layers

LayerWhatPeak levelRule
1 · BedRain, water, wind, heat hum, crowd murmur−24 dBRuns under the whole scene. Fade in 0.3 s, never cut hard.
2 · ActionBricks, steps, splashes, bounces, grips−12 dBOne sound per visible contact, placed on the exact frame of contact.
3 · UILabel ticks, text pop, typewriter−16 dBEvery label and text line gets a sound on the frame it appears.
4 · PunctuationRiser, sub drop, impact, chime−6 dBOnce per reel only, on the payoff. This is the moment people remember.
S1

Sync to the frame.Contact sounds start on the contact frame. Whooshes start 2–3 frames before the movement they describe.

S2

Give each character a sonic identity.The winner's actions are crisp and bright (high-passed). The loser's are duller and quieter (low-passed, −4 dB). The ear hears who is winning.

S3

Silence before the payoff.Duck everything for 6–10 frames just before the punctuation hit. Contrast makes the hit land.

S4

Never repeat the exact same sample twice in a row.Shift pitch ±1 to 3 semitones and volume ±2 dB for each repetition (bricks, footsteps, bounces). Identical repeats sound robotic.

S5

Leave room for the music.Keep the overall mix under −14 LUFS. In Instagram, set the original audio to about 60–70% and the music track to 20–35%, so the effects stay audible under the beat.

S6

Pick music by mood, not by trend alone.Slow piano or ambient pads suit reflective reels (umbrella, climb). Dark phonk or a steady beat suits action reels (bricks, discipline). Start the music on the hook frame.

S7

Export.48 kHz, stereo AAC 320 kbps, baked into the MP4. Pan the winner 15% right and the loser 15% left when they're on opposite sides of the screen.

07 · Text

On-screen text rules

T1

Labels from frame 1.Character labels are on screen from the very first frame so the scroll-stopper already makes sense.

T2

Closing line only in the last 25%.It names the lesson after the viewer has already felt it. Typewriter reveal, 2 frames per letter, with ui_typewriter_line.

T3

Two-part lines hit harder."DON'T FOLLOW BLINDLY." then a beat, then "OR YOU'LL PAY THE CONSEQUENCES." Shows as two reveals, and the second gets the hit.

T4

Parallel structure."DON'T RELY ON MOTIVATION / RELY ON DISCIPLINE". "SAME 5 MINUTES. DIFFERENT DAY." Repetition with one word changed is the most quotable form.

T5

No hashtags, emoji or handles in the video.Those go in the caption.

09 · Posting

Posting rules

  • Cover: the payoff frame with both labels visible. It should make sense in the profile grid.
  • Caption formula: a short line that makes people tag someone, then a question. For example: "Which one are you right now? 👇" or "Send this to the friend who keeps snoozing."
  • Hashtags: 5 to 8. Mix one big (#motivation), three mid (#disciplineovermotivation, #selfimprovement, #mindsetshift) and two niche (#stickmananimation, #minimalistanimation).
  • Timing: 7–9 am or 8–10 pm local time, when people scroll before work or before sleep.
  • Cadence: 3 per week minimum, same visual system every time, so the page reads as one series.
  • First hour: reply to every comment. Pin the best one.
  • Track: watch time over 100%, saves and shares. Those three tell you which metaphors to repeat.

11 · Quality check

Before you post

  • Lesson guessable with the sound off and labels hidden.
  • Both characters on screen in frame 1, with labels visible.
  • Same conditions for both. Only the choice differs.
  • Red is used on one object only.
  • Everything important sits inside the safe zone (y 380–1480).
  • Every visible contact has a sound, synced to the frame.
  • One punctuation hit only, on the payoff, with silence just before it.
  • Closing line of 8 words or fewer, arriving in the last 25%.
  • Loops cleanly from the last frame back to the first.
  • Total length 10–14 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps, audio at −14 LUFS or below.
