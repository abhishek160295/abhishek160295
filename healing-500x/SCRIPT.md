# What If Your Body Healed 500X Faster? — Narration Script

You slip with a cutter.

Deep slice on your thumb.

You brace for the sting.

The bleeding stops before the first drop hits the floor.

The edges knit like a zipper.

Half a second later, no pink line.

You burn your tongue on hot soup.

**LEO:** [sharp inhale] Ouch!

One blink, and the burn is gone.

You sprain your ankle on a fence.

Your foot swells.

It clicks.

It heals mid-air, before you even touch the grass.

**LEO:** [gasps] Ah!

**JAY:** [shocked] Bro, did your ankle just fix itself?

You bite pizza, your tongue is fine.

A normal paper cut takes seven days to heal.

A broken bone takes six weeks.

You?

Five hundred times faster.

A cut closes in eighteen seconds.

A fractured bone knits in sixty seconds flat.

But your cells follow one absolute rule.

Your body heals in whatever position it sits in.

If a bone knits crooked, it locks crooked forever.

You have exactly sixty seconds before your cells lock the bone.

You heal fast, but you must align it right.

So what if your body healed five hundred times faster?

At first, it feels like magic.

Skateboarding wipeouts mean nothing to you.

You crash into concrete at thirty kilometers an hour.

Scraped knees, gone before you stand up.

Torn shirt, still torn, but the skin underneath is brand new.

At the dentist, the drill hits a nerve.

You flinch.

Before the doctor even reaches for a filling, enamel regrows over the cavity.

[whispering] Wait, where did the hole go?

Plus, no scars, no bruises, zero recovery time.

You do a thousand pushups in gym class.

Muscle fibers tear.

They rebuild while you're still on the floor.

You stand up stronger in four seconds.

Coach looks at his board.

**JAY:** [stunned] Are you even human?

Minus the caloric tax.

Rapid cell division is not free.

Building millions of new cells in seconds burns insane energy.

One broken bone still costs four thousand calories, now you pay it in one minute.

A deep cut costs eight hundred.

By noon, you're on your fifth lunch.

Jay stares at your burger wrappers.

**JAY:** Mate, are you eating us out of house and home?

**LEO:** [mouth full] Ah!

**LEO:** I'm not greedy.

**LEO:** My ribs are knitting.

The catch?

If a bone snaps and you don't set it straight, it fuses crooked in sixty seconds.

You trip on the stairs.

You fracture your wrist.

You don't straighten it in time.

Click.

Your wrist heals at a forty-five degree angle.

**LEO:** [in pain] Ah, ow!

Jay grabs your hand.

He snaps it straight before the sixty-second mark.

He holds it tight while you scream into a pillow.

**LEO:** [screams] Aaah!

**JAY:** Hold still!

**LEO:** [through gritted teeth] I am holding!

Then comes the hidden cost. Metabolic fever.

When your body repairs a major injury, billions of cells divide at once.

Your core temperature spikes to forty-two degrees Celsius.

You sweat through your bed in forty seconds.

Your heart slams at two hundred and twenty beats per minute to feed the repair.

Even worse, immune hyperdrive.

Your body treats every splinter, bee sting, and tattoo ink droplet like a full-scale war.

A tiny splinter swells your finger like a balloon.

It fights the wood.

It spits it out in four seconds.

Can you get piercings?

No.

Tattoos?

Never.

Your skin fights the world.

Your best friend Jay understands.

He becomes your human medical team.

He carries a carbon fiber upper body harness in his backpack, two padded clamps for the collarbone, and a forearm rig.

If a bone snaps, he has sixty seconds to clamp your shoulder and arm.

He even makes a custom wristband with four words in the leather. Keep it straight, Leo.

Your name is Leo.

You wear that wristband everywhere.

You make a joke out of it.

**LEO:** [laughing] What if I break my leg?

**JAY:** Do not let me sit down.

**JAY:** I will clamp you to a lamppost if I have to.

Then comes a stormy Tuesday afternoon.

You and Jay walk past an old warehouse.

An iron beam falls toward a young girl.

You jump without thinking.

You shove her away.

The iron beam smashes onto your right shoulder and arm.

Crack.

Collarbone and forearm shatter into fragments.

The pain is blinding, but hyperhealing kicks in, five hundred times faster.

Cells start knitting shattered bones in their crushed, twisted position.

You look down.

You have less than sixty seconds.

If your arm fuses under this beam, your bones lock into a mangled ball forever.

Will you lose the arm?

Yes, unless you lift the beam, and you do not have super strength.

You look around. Panic.

A heavy iron pipe lies near your feet.

You jam the pipe under the beam like a lever over a concrete block.

You throw your full body weight onto the lever.

**LEO:** [straining] Aaagh!

Pain explodes in your left shoulder as the joint pops out of its socket, but the leverage lifts the beam three inches.

You yank your crushed right arm free.

You collapse onto wet pavement.

Your left shoulder is dislocated, and at five hundred times speed, it's already starting to lock out of place.

Jay drops to his knees.

First, he grabs your left arm.

He pops the shoulder back into its socket before it locks wrong.

Click.

He pulls out the upper body harness.

He straps the chest rig over your collarbone.

He clamps your right forearm.

**LEO:** [cries out] Ah!

**JAY:** Hold still!

**JAY:** [desperate] Leo!

He ratchets the straps tight.

He pulls shattered fragments into perfect alignment.

Click. Click. Click.

**LEO:** [exhales sharply] Ahh.

Hypercells flood in.

Forty-eight seconds of agony.

Bones knit straight.

You sit up.

**LEO:** [weakly] Is the girl safe?

**JAY:** Yes.

**JAY:** She's with her mom.

Jay slaps your good shoulder.

**JAY:** [smiling] Keep it straight, Leo.

You look at your wristband.

You laugh through tears.

Is any of this real?

Humans can't heal like this yet, but nature already runs complex regeneration programs.

The axolotl salamander regrows entire limbs, tails, and even heart tissue over weeks or months, without scars.

In nineteen ninety-eight, scientists at the Wistar Institute studied the MRL mouse, a strain with remarkable cartilage and tissue repair.

Their secret?

Biological pathways linked to enhanced tissue regeneration.

Could human cells ever switch these pathways on?

That night, you sit on your bed eating your sixth meal.

Your phone vibrates.

A video clip plays.

A black van sits outside your house.

A woman in a gray coat looks into the camera.

**AGENT:** We have a boy who flies.

**AGENT:** We have a boy who stops time.
